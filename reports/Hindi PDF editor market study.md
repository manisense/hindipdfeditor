# Win the Hindi problems giants ignore

hindipdfeditor.com should not try to beat iLovePDF and Adobe at generic PDF tools. It should own the jobs those tools get wrong for Hindi, then use cheap, high-frequency sarkari utilities and a WhatsApp-native install path to bring users in. The evidence lines up behind this. **Adobe's own community shows matras scattering when existing Hindi is edited, from about 2019 to late 2025, and an Adobe Community Manager confirmed in September 2026 that Acrobat's built-in OCR does not support Hindi** ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-15/make-hindi-language-available-in-text-recognition-ocr-in-adobe-acrobat-1640655)). No surveyed competitor, global or Indian, shows evidence that it correctly replaces existing Devanagari text inside a PDF or converts a KrutiDev PDF to Unicode in place. On demand, the **legacy-font conversion cluster (Mangal↔KrutiDev, DevLys, AMS, Shree Lipi) adds up to roughly 400K+ India searches a month, and its results pages are held by sites with Authority Scores of 10–42** ([Semrush: rajbhasha.net](https://www.semrush.com/website/rajbhasha.net/overview/); [Semrush: hindityping.info](https://semrush.com/website/hindityping.info/overview)). That makes it the largest Hindi-specific cluster a new site can realistically win. Government policy is pushing offices from KrutiDev to Unicode through 2026–27, so this demand should grow. The generic shell ("jpg to pdf" ~7.5M, "pdf to word" ~5M a month in India) is locked by incumbents, and the price floor is now zero because Adobe made 25+ online tools free in India. Free local tools therefore have to be the acquisition layer. Revenue has to come from Hindi-specific AI (OCR, translation) sold as UPI passes and from a pro tier for heavy users such as CSC operators and typists. The top-ranked moves are: (1) a full legacy-font conversion hub, including the missing Unicode→KrutiDev direction; (2) proving and publicising correct Hindi editing, and deciding on searchable "Plan B" output; (3) a sarkari upload kit (target-size compression, photo/signature resizer, PDF↔JPG); (4) an installable PWA with a share target for PDFs that arrive on WhatsApp; and (5) metered Sarvam OCR and translation. All search volumes are Semrush model estimates, and no competitor was tested hands-on, so several rankings depend on checks flagged below.

## Nobody edits existing Hindi correctly, and Acrobat cannot OCR it

The best-documented failure belongs to the market leader. In Adobe Community threads from 2025, users say editing any Hindi PDF in Acrobat Pro makes "all the Hindi matras... get scattered", that this "occurs with all Hindi PDFs, regardless of which font is used", and that it has lasted "for many years". Volunteers offered only settings checks, which did not help ([Adobe Community, Sep–Oct 2025](https://community.adobe.com/questions-9/hindi-language-pdf-page-edit-problem-1304863); [Adobe Community, 8 Oct 2025](https://community.adobe.com/t5/acrobat-discussions/hindi-language-all-pdf-edit-problem-edit-is-mismatch-not-accurate/td-p/15538131)). One community reply states plainly that Acrobat does not support editing Hindi text yet ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/acrobat-discussions/pdf-document-editing-hindi-text-editing-bug-in-adobe-acrobat-dc/td-p/15398453)). The workarounds suggested in these threads are to convert text to outlines, which makes it uneditable, or to OCR the page and redo the work in Word or Google Docs ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/acrobat-discussions/issues-in-editing-pdf-s-in-quot-hindi-quot/m-p/14792384)). The same weakness shows up across Adobe's PDF stack: Express "Save to PDF", Hindi letterhead export, Gujarati in Reader's Reading Mode and Punjabi in form fields ([Express bug report](https://community.adobe.com/bug-reports-328/adobe-express-save-to-pdf-doesn-t-support-hindi-perfectly-1468805); [form inputs](https://community.adobe.com/questions-9/problems-rendering-punjabi-and-gujarati-text-in-pdf-form-inputs-1552298)). A pattern that spans this many products points to a deep shaping problem, which is unlikely to be fixed quickly. Text extraction is broken too. A June 2026 report shows that even correctly displayed Unicode Mangal PDFs change conjuncts on copy and search, and Adobe staff replied that "text extraction and search features currently have limited support for Indian languages" ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-9/hindi-unicode-pdf-search-and-copy-paste-issue-1629373)).

OCR is the second gap. **Acrobat's OCR has no Hindi. Adobe's Community Manager called it "a product limitation" and sends users to the separate Adobe Scan app** ([Adobe Community, Sep 2026](https://community.adobe.com/questions-15/make-hindi-language-available-in-text-recognition-ocr-in-adobe-acrobat-1640655)). iLovePDF is the strongest global rival on AI. Its Translate PDF tool, launched 25 February 2026, recognises 120+ languages including Hindi, translates in both directions with a "Keep layout" mode, and substitutes "visually similar fonts" when the original is missing ([iLovePDF blog](https://www.ilovepdf.com/hi/blog/introducing-translate-pdf-ai-pdf-translation)). Its pricing page places translation and OCR-to-Office behind Premium, which conflicts with the blog's one-a-day free allowance ([iLovePDF pricing](https://www.ilovepdf.com/hi/pricing)). The free Hindi OCR most Indians already reach for is Google Drive's "Open with Google Docs", which detects Hindi automatically but outputs plain text under the image, and Google's help page caps files at 2 MB ([Google Drive Help](https://support.google.com/drive/answer/176692)). UPDF and PDFgear both say they can edit Hindi and OCR it, but only on their own marketing pages. PDFgear's page even admits that "OCR technology for Hindi isn't as advanced as for Latin languages" and that non-Unicode encodings "can corrupt text" ([PDFgear](https://www.pdfgear.com/pdf-converter/how-to-convert-hindi-pdf-to-word.htm); [UPDF](https://updf.com/edit-pdf/hindi-pdf-editor/)). No documentation was found, either way, on whether iLovePDF Edit, Smallpdf, Sejda, Foxit or PDFescape can edit Hindi. The absence of complaints there more likely reflects low usage than better shaping.

India-specific competitors are small and fragmented. On Play, a search for "Hindi PDF editor" has no Hindi-specialist app at scale. The owner's own app shows 100+ downloads, and the large generic editors (10M+ installs) are mostly criticised for paywalls, not Hindi ([Play: xsdev](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details/PDF_text_editor_Edit_PDF?id=com.xsdev.pdfreader.pdfeditor.pdf.document&hl=en_US); [Play: Desygner](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details/PDF_Editor_Edit_Everything?id=com.desygner.pdf&hl=en_US)). Some Android editors admit Indic limits in their own listings. One says PDF text uses Latin letters and "other scripts become ?" ([Easy PDF Edit](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.manthan.pdf)). Indian web tools that claim Hindi support (IndiaPDF Editor, eofficetool, RVS, tool-web.dev) mean typing new Devanagari text or running Hindi OCR, not replacing existing lines ([IndiaPDF Editor](https://indiapdfeditor.com/); [eofficetool](https://www.eofficetool.com/pdf-editor)). **IndiaPDF Editor is the closest web rival by positioning. It offers ten Indian scripts, Hindi+English OCR and free Premium for signed-in users until 31 January 2027, but it names no operator and does not explain where its AI features send data** ([IndiaPDF Editor](https://indiapdfeditor.com/)). RajTool is the strongest legacy-font player. It converts KrutiDev, DevLys and Chanakya in both directions, accepts DOCX, and ships Google Workspace and Microsoft add-ins, but no source shows it converting a PDF while keeping the layout ([RajTool](https://www.rajtool.com/krutidev-converter); [Workspace Marketplace](https://workspace.google.com/marketplace/app/unicode_converter/64787471443)). Sarvam (Akshar, Vision) and Bhashini overlap on Hindi OCR and document translation, but they target developers, enterprises and government, and they do not edit PDFs. They are more likely suppliers than rivals ([Sarvam Akshar](https://www.sarvam.ai/products/akshar); [News on AIR: CAG-BHASHINI Anuvaad](https://newsonair.gov.in/dibd-cag-launch-ai-enabled-cag-bhashini-anuvaad-for-document-translation/)).

## Legacy-font conversion is the biggest Hindi cluster a new site can win

India's PDF search demand comes in three bands. The top band is generic English utilities, all held by iLovePDF at #1 and Smallpdf at #2–3: **"jpg to pdf" ~7.48M, "pdf to word" ~5M, "pdf to jpg" ~4.09M, and "merge pdf" and "word to pdf" ~3.35M each per month** ([Semrush: ilovepdf.com](https://www.semrush.com/website/ilovepdf.com/overview/); [Semrush: smallpdf.com](https://www.semrush.com/website/smallpdf.com/overview/)). India is iLovePDF's largest country, at about 18% of its 303M October 2026 visits ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/website/ilovepdf.com/overview/)). The middle band is sarkari utilities: size-target compression ("compress pdf to 100kb", "pdf 200 kb") and exam photo or signature resizing. No India volume was visible for these, but 11zon and pi7 drew 5–10M visits a month in early 2025, largely on these tasks, and Adobe India built dedicated 100KB, 150KB, 200KB and 250KB compression pages ([Semrush: 11zon](https://cw.semrush.com/website/11zon.com/overview); [Adobe IN 100KB](https://www.adobe.com/in/acrobat/online/compress-pdf-100kb.html)). The third band is Hindi legacy-font conversion. It is measured and sizeable, and incumbents ignore it:

|---|---|---|

| kruti dev to mangal | 60,500 | rajbhasha.net #8 |

Sources: [Semrush: rajbhasha.net](https://www.semrush.com/website/rajbhasha.net/overview/); [Semrush: hindityping.info](https://semrush.com/website/hindityping.info/overview); [Semrush: krutidevunicode.com](https://ja.semrush.com/website/krutidevunicode.com/overview). A figure of ~246K for "krutidev to unicode" appeared only in a 2025 search summary and could not be confirmed on the fetched pages, so the cluster total of 400K+ leaves it out. Three details matter for the product. First, **the two largest terms ("mangal to kruti" 135K and "unicode to krutidev" 33.1K) are the Unicode→KrutiDev direction, which the product does not offer**. Typists and DTP operators still need legacy output for SSC typing exams and older portals. SSC CHSL's official typing instructions still list Hindi KrutiDev and Remington layouts ([SSC CR 2023](https://mail.ssc-cr.org/noticeboard/CHSL%202023%20Tier%20II%20Typing_Candidate%20Instructions_%20v_3-_10_2023.pdf)). Second, people type these queries in Latin script and with misspellings, so pages must target "kurtidev10" and "kruti dev" variants. Third, every visible Hindi-font keyword shows a CPC of $0, which signals little ad value. These keywords are worth having for user acquisition, not for ad revenue.

The Hindi-specific PDF tasks themselves ("hindi pdf edit", broken-matra queries, Hindi OCR, Hindi PDF to Word) are the smallest band by volume, but they carry the highest intent. The earlier SEO audit found these results pages filled by Adobe Community threads, Quora and vendor listicles, with no result that directly fixes broken letters ([UPDF listicle](https://updf.com/edit-pdf/hindi-pdf-editor/); [Quora](https://www.quora.com/How-do-I-copy-the-Hindi-text-from-PDF-when-it-is-displaying-some-garbage-English-values-while-copying)). Language preference skews toward Hindi output, not Hindi queries. **98% of India's 870M internet users accessed Indic-language content in 2024, and 57% of urban users prefer it** ([Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/india-news/india-s-internet-users-to-exceed-900-mn-in-2025-driven-by-indic-languages-125011600835_1.html)). Yet every measured PDF keyword is in Latin script. Users search for tools in English or Hinglish and want correct Hindi in the result. About a third of Google queries in India are spoken ([Inc42, 2023](https://inc42.com/?p=403119)), which favours natural-language Hinglish FAQs ("pdf ka size kaise kam kare").

Seasonality evidence is indirect. 11zon's visits fell from 10.2M in February to 5.6M in April 2025, which fits exam-form windows ([Semrush](https://cw.semrush.com/website/11zon.com/overview)). Hindi typing and converter sites peaked in September 2026 and dipped in October ([Semrush: hindityping.info](https://semrush.com/website/hindityping.info/overview); [Semrush: krutidevunicode.com](https://ja.semrush.com/website/krutidevunicode.com/overview)). A plausible but untested cause is Hindi Diwas (14 September) and Hindi Pakhwada in government offices, followed by the festival season. Content and launches for the conversion hub should land before September, and sarkari pages before major recruitment windows. Google Keyword Planner and Search Console were not accessed, so these numbers are modelled estimates to confirm before committing heavy effort.

## Every workaround today is a multi-tool chain

The core pain is not missing Hindi fonts. The text stored in the PDF is wrong. Legacy fonts such as KrutiDev store "क" as "d", so copied text comes out as Latin gibberish. Generic converters "assumed English content" and failed, and pdfplumber and PyMuPDF return garbled English for legacy fonts and nothing for scans ([Digital Orientalist, Dec 2025](https://digitalorientalist.com/2025/12/02/why-extracting-hindi-text-from-pdfs-is-so-much-harder-than-english-and-how-you-can-do-it/); [DocSet](https://docset.in/blog/hindi-font-conversion-guide)). Even proper Unicode PDFs garble on PDF-to-Word export in Acrobat ([Adobe Community: Mangal export](https://community.adobe.com/questions-9/pdf-export-file-not-correct-contains-mangal-hindi-unicode-font-1285751)). KrutiDev-to-Unicode conversion is not a one-to-one swap. Matras must be reordered, which is why simple macros fail ([ProZ](https://ben.proz.com/post/3001315)). So today's workaround is a chain: copy the garbled text, paste it into a converter site, paste the result into Word, or convert pages to JPG and OCR them, or retype everything. The only "real" fix staff offer is to ask the publisher to re-export the file ([Mozilla support](https://support.mozilla.org/zu/questions/1402379)). **A single flow that detects whether a page is legacy, Unicode or scanned, then converts or OCRs it, then lets the user edit, removes three or four steps.** The product already has most of this flow for KrutiDev-family pages.

Policy is turning this into growing demand. A Department of Official Language programme for 2026–27, cited in an institute circular, requires central offices to adopt Unicode Hindi and blames KrutiDev for formatting loss ([RFRI circular](https://rfri.icfre.org/circular-files/circular38.pdf)). A CAG circular of 8 April 2026 says bilingual e-Office work should be converted to Unicode ([CAG](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/ae_circulars_office_orders/CircularsOfficeOrders-069d89fcba5ea81-97780071.pdf)). Rajasthan High Court e-filing requires Unicode Hindi and bars KrutiDev and DevLys ([Rajasthan HC](https://hcraj.nic.in/hcraj/Allfiles/GuidelinesForEfiling.pdf)). Several courts also require OCR-searchable PDFs or PDF/A at 300 DPI. Delhi High Court raised its e-filing cap to 300 MB in February 2026 ([Delhi HC](https://delhihighcourt.nic.in/files/2026-02/announcements/16it.pdf)). That last requirement bears directly on the product's biggest known gap. **Edited Hindi words saved as images are not searchable, which conflicts with court rules that want searchable filings.** This makes the owner's pending "Plan B" decision on shaped-glyph export a market question, not only an engineering one.

Six user groups recur. Exam aspirants are the largest by volume and need small, deadline-driven utilities. SSC photos must be 20–50 KB and signatures 10–20 KB, and a missing date on the photo is called the most common rejection reason. These figures come from coaching sites, which disagree with each other on dimensions ([PW](https://www.pw.live/ssc/exams/ssc-cgl-photo-and-signature-size-2026); [Testbook](https://testbook.com/exam-photo-crop-resize-tool)). Candidates post anxious questions about wrong-size uploads ([Careers360](https://www.careers360.com/question-hello-i-have-mistakenly-uploaded-signature-picture-of-wrong-dimensions-in-ssc-chsl-will-my-form-be-rejected)). Government typists and office staff live inside the KrutiDev/Unicode split. Lawyers and court filers face Unicode-only rules and size caps. Publishers using InDesign with Mangal hit extraction bugs. Researchers and translators need clean Hindi text out of PDFs. Panchayat, school and accounts record-keepers fight scanned registers that lose their table structure in plain OCR ([dev.to](https://dev.to/algozasolutions/india-runs-on-scanned-documents-why-cant-we-convert-them-properly-4kcd)). Cyber cafés and Jan Seva Kendras sell form filling as a service, which makes them repeat power users of resize, compress and fill tools ([IndiaMART listing](https://m.indiamart.com/cybercafe-arambagh)). One demand signal must be handled carefully. Students search for how to fix a wrong name or photo on an admit card, and every legitimate source points to the issuing body's correction window or helpdesk ([PW: JEE Main](https://www.pw.live/iit-jee/exams/jee-main-2026-session-2-admit-card-errors-what-to-do-if-any-detail-is-wrong); [Careers360: NEET](https://medicine.careers360.com/articles/what-to-do-if-name-is-wrong-in-nta-neet-admit-card-2026)). Pages that capture this traffic should route users there, in line with the project rule never to guide edits to authority-issued documents.

Two of these pain points are weakly evidenced. Reddit, Quora, Play reviews and YouTube comments did not surface in search, so the student voice is under-represented. None of the KB limits was confirmed against ssc.gov.in or upsc.gov.in, and UPSC ranges conflict across guides (20–300 KB versus 20–100 KB) ([Vajiram & Ravi](https://vajiramandravi.com/upsc-exam/upsc-photo-and-signature-guidelines/)). Any exam preset must be checked against the official notice for that cycle.

## Free giants set a zero price floor, so charge for Hindi AI

Distribution should start from how Indian phones are actually used. **Android holds 93.9% of India's mobile OS share** ([Statcounter, Sep 2026](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/india)). Data averages 25.5 GB per subscriber per month at about ₹8/GB ([TelecomTalk / TRAI](https://telecomtalk.info/revenue-per-gb-data-drops-fy26-trai/1012551/)), so download size matters less than device memory. Counterpoint expects the sub-₹15,000 segment to shrink as memory costs rise, and shipments fell about 10% in the June 2026 quarter ([FoneArena](https://www.fonearena.com/blog/474743/india-smartphone-market-2025-counterpoint.html); [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/technology/smartphone-shipments-in-india-drop-10-pc-in-june-quarter-over-memory-prices-enn26071701740)). This argues for testing in-browser rendering on 3–4 GB devices. India is WhatsApp's largest market ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/14/whatsapps-biggest-market-is-becoming-its-toughest-test)), and Chrome's Web Share Target lets an *installed* PWA appear in Android's share sheet for PDFs and images. The manifest needs a `share_target` entry with POST multipart. The same behaviour carries into a Trusted Web Activity wrapper on Play from Chrome 86 ([Chrome Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/capabilities/web-apis/web-share-target); [TWA share target](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/android/trusted-web-activity/web-share-target)). With the native Android app on hold, a TWA is a cheap way to keep a Play presence that reuses the web editor. The usual Indian PWA precedent is Flipkart Lite: 70% higher conversion among Add-to-Homescreen arrivals and 40% higher re-engagement ([web.dev](https://web.dev/case-studies/flipkart)). Those numbers are from 2016 e-commerce, so treat them only as directional.

The other channels are people and answer engines. **India has about 100M weekly ChatGPT users** ([TechCrunch, Feb 2026](https://www.techcrunch.com/2026/02/15/india-has-100m-weekly-active-chatgpt-users-sam-altman-says/)), and Sensor Tower counted 118M monthly Gemini users in January 2026 ([Digit](https://www.digit.in/features/general/chatgpt-is-king-of-ai-in-india-gemini-perplexity-lag-far-behind-in-daily-users.html)). Quotable, accurate pages are therefore a real channel. Chat assistants can OCR and translate, but they cannot return a layout-preserving PDF with correct Devanagari. There are about **5–5.5 lakh operational CSCs** ([IMPRI](https://www.impriindia.com/?p=64505); [Tripura Info](https://tripurainfo.com/NationalNewDetails.aspx?nId=7355)), a concentrated network of operators who handle sarkari PDFs daily. The earlier audit found them in Telegram and Facebook groups, and found toolkits already marketed to them ([Telegram: Smart CSC Help](https://t.me/s/smartcschelp); [Photocopywala](https://photocopywala.in/cyber-cafe-tools/)). Hindi YouTube tutorials already rank for "Hindi pdf file kaise edit kare" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CRNoYjPQA5g)). A side-by-side video, showing matras scattering in Acrobat and staying intact in the product, is the most persuasive asset the product could make. None of these channels could be quantified for this category in this research.

On money, the free baseline is very high. **Adobe offers unlimited use of 25+ online tools in India with a free account, including Edit PDF, compression up to 2 GB and PDF to Word, but excludes OCR and AI** ([Adobe India](https://www.adobe.com/in/acrobat/online.html); [StartupTalky, Nov 2026](https://startuptalky.com/adobe-acrobat-online-free/)). iLovePDF Premium costs ₹500/month or ₹2,900/year (₹242/month), with translation, OCR-to-Office and 2,000 AI credits behind the paywall ([iLovePDF pricing](https://www.ilovepdf.com/hi/pricing)). Smallpdf Pro is listed at ₹979 excluding GST on TechJockey ([TechJockey](https://www.techjockey.com/detail/smallpdf)). Sejda's $5 non-renewing Week Pass is the clearest precedent for a pass model ([Sejda](https://www.sejda.com/pricing)). Indian users skew toward free and ad-supported products. AppsFlyer puts India's share of paying Android users 11% below the global level ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-apac-india-app-boom-revenue-problem/)). But UPI AutoPay mandates crossed 1.27 billion by November 2025, with no repeat approval needed up to ₹15,000 ([Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/blog/master-recurring-payments-upi-autopay-guide/)), so the payment rails exist for both passes and subscriptions. Ads are a poor fit. India RPM estimates run ₹40–165 per 1,000 pageviews in most niches ([upGrowth](https://upgrowth.in/?p=48732)), and third-party ad scripts would undercut the local-processing privacy story.

Unit costs point to the same model. **Sarvam lists Document AI parsing at ₹0.50 a page and translation at ₹2 per 1,000 characters (₹5 per 1,000 billable characters for its Document Translation API), all before GST** ([Sarvam pricing](https://docs.sarvam.ai/api/getting-started/pricing.md)). Assuming about 2,500 characters on a dense Hindi page, OCR costs about ₹0.59 a page with GST and translation about ₹5.90 through the text API. A ₹49 pass covering 20 OCR pages and 5 translated pages would cost about ₹35 in API fees, so translation needs ₹99+ pricing or tight caps, while OCR can be partly subsidised. Sarvam's document limits conflict across its own pages (10 pages per request versus 500 pages) ([Sarvam docs](https://docs.sarvam.ai/api/api-guides-tutorials/document-intelligence/overview.md)). Its claim that Vision scores 95.91 on Hindi against Gemini 3 Pro's 95.12 comes from its own benchmark ([AIM](https://analyticsindiamag.com/ai-news/sarvam-vision-beats-gemini-gpt-on-indic-ocr-across-22-indian-languages)). Live accuracy on the project fixture should decide which provider goes first. A workable mix is free local tools forever, UPI passes of roughly ₹19–99 for AI OCR and translation, and an annual pro tier around ₹499–999 for CSC operators, typists and offices. These price points are estimates that need testing, and there is no India utility-SaaS conversion benchmark, so plan on 0.5–2% of monthly users paying until real data arrives.

Two competitive watch items affect timing. IndiaPDF Editor's free Premium runs until 31 January 2027 and is likely to convert to paid after that, which will test how price-sensitive the same audience is. iLovePDF's Hindi translation sets the bar a translate feature must beat, but whether its "Keep layout" output shapes Devanagari correctly is untested, and it is the first comparison to run with the fixture.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "#1 Hindi PDF editor" means. The Hindi-editing query is small, and the competition that matters is not over who has the most tools. It is over who sits at the moment a Hindi document turns out to be wrong: garbled on copy, scattered on edit, the wrong font for a Unicode-only court, or too big for an exam portal. Policy is pushing millions of KrutiDev-era files into that moment through 2027, and the incumbents have either declined the problem (Acrobat's OCR) or never claimed it (in-place legacy conversion). The product already holds the hardest piece, correct shaping plus KrutiDev PDF decoding. The cheapest gains are on the edges: a two-way text converter, a public benchmark, and a share-sheet install.

The biggest strategic risk is self-inflicted. Saving new words as images leaves the product's own output failing the searchability that courts and offices ask for, and that its users complain is broken elsewhere. Until Plan B is decided, public copy should state this limit plainly, as the project rules already require. The next three checks will sharpen the ranking most: Google Keyword Planner volumes for the sarkari and OCR clusters, a fixture test of iLovePDF, Smallpdf and IndiaPDF Editor, and a live Sarvam-versus-Gemini accuracy and cost run.
