How to Translate a Hindi PDF to English (and Keep the Layout)

A Hindi government circular lands in your inbox and your manager reads only English. Or you've got an English notice that needs to go out in Hindi. How you translate it depends on one question: do you just need to understand it, or do you need a document that looks like the original?

By Manish · Updated 25 September 2026 · about 5 minutes to read

Short answer

Just need to understand it? Copy the text into Google Translate, or use Google Lens on a scan. Need an editable English version? Upload the PDF to Google Translate's document mode or open it in Google Docs. Need the translated text back on the original page? Use Hindi PDF Editor's translate tool: it detects the language, translates each line after you confirm, and places the translation where the original line was.

Option 1: Copy and paste into a translator

Select the Hindi text, copy it, and paste it into Google Translate or any translator you trust. It's fast and fine for understanding a page. The catch: if the pasted Hindi comes out as jumbled letters, the PDF's text layer is broken, and the translation will be nonsense too. In that case, use option 2 or 4.

Option 2: Google Lens for scans and photos

For a scanned notice or a photo of a page, Google Lens reads the printed Devanagari and translates it on screen. It's the quickest way to get the gist of a scanned document on your phone. You get the meaning, not a document you can send.

Option 3: Translate the whole document into an editable file

Google Translate's document mode, or opening the PDF with Google Docs and then using its translate feature, gives you an editable English version. It works well for text-heavy documents. Complex layouts such as tables, stamps and two-column forms usually come out rearranged, so expect to tidy it up.

Option 4: Keep the original page and swap the text

Sometimes the look of the page matters: a form, a certificate or a notice with a letterhead, where readers expect the original layout. That's what the translate tool in Hindi PDF Editor is for.

1. Open the PDF

The tool reads the text lines on each page. For scanned pages it uses text recognition.

2. Check the detected direction and confirm

It works out whether the page is Hindi or English and translates the other way. Pass the quick security check and confirm. Only then is the text sent for translation.

3. Download and proofread

Each translated line is placed where the original was. Click "Translate & download" for the new PDF. Your original file is not changed.

Where it struggles: English usually runs longer than Hindi, so a long translation in a small box gets small type. Handwriting and very faint scans aren't read reliably. Legal and technical terms may come out literal. Read the result before you send it.

Which one should you use?

  • To understand a page: copy-paste or Google Lens.
  • To edit the translation further: Google Docs or Translate's document mode.
  • To share a page that looks like the original: translate in place, then proofread.

Other questions people ask

Is machine translation accurate enough for legal or official use?

It's good for understanding a document and for a first draft. For anything you will sign, submit or rely on legally, have a person who reads both languages check it, especially names, amounts, dates and legal terms.

Is my PDF uploaded when I translate?

In Hindi PDF Editor the PDF file isn't uploaded. After you confirm, the detected text lines are sent to our Gemini-based translation service, and for scanned pages the page image is sent for text recognition. The original file is never changed.

Why does the copied Hindi turn into gibberish before I can translate it?

The PDF probably uses a legacy font like Kruti Dev or has a broken text map. See our guide on fixing broken Hindi text to find out which.
